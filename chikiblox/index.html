          How to play

          • One structure, six downs. Every player falls a different way. On your turn the faces you can stand on turn pale.
          • Build. By default placing is optional: each turn you can place one of your pieces and one stair, in either order, or skip either. A setting can make both required, and another lets you take a stair away instead of placing one. Pick it and it appears near you with a gumball. Drag an arrow to slide it, drag or tap an arc to turn it, then place it. A piece must touch the structure on at least two squares. A stair's stepped sides don't count; its flat back and bottom do, and so does a full (two-stair) block.
          • Stairs. Stairs are mirror-symmetric, like Escher's: the player standing on the floor and the player standing on the wall behind can both climb them. A flat side has to sit on a cube face. Crossing a stair's steps costs one step and you can't stop on them, but its flat back and bottom are square faces you can stand on like any cube. A second stair fills a stair's cube.
          • Walk. 3 steps unless the table changes it. Walk on the flat and drop down one cube (the table can change the drop limit). Going up needs a stair, or you can spend your whole walk scaling one cube straight up a side. You can build over someone's head, but never in their cube. You can walk past other players but can't stop on them. You can't walk through whoever is after you: they block your way (in free-for-all that's everyone). With secret targets you can't tell, so you can try, but if they turn out to be hunting you they grab you and you're out. Seal someone in, so the empty space they're in has no way out (in their own gravity, over their head too), and they are boxed in and out. That counts even if the pocket holds two players. The last gap has to be closed by a block: stairs already on the board count as walls, but a stair placed this turn can't help, unless you're out of blocks. A single stair only walls off the sides its flat back and bottom face; its stepped half is a gap. Two stairs in one cube make a full wall. In the Targets game you can only box in your target.
          • Taking people out. In the default Targets game everyone can see who is after whom (a setting makes it secret). You can only take out your own target, and nobody can be taken out before their first turn (in free-for-all, anyone can). Reach: if you could end your walk in someone's square, they're out. Count it exactly as if the square were empty: your steps, the drop limit, stairs and ledges, and there must be a floor under that square in your gravity, so you could actually stand there. Dropping onto someone from a ledge is just reaching their square. If you walk in from the side and there's an empty edge beyond them, they get shoved off it. Corner: if they have no free square to move to, ending your walk next to them is enough. Box in: seal off the empty space around them, sides and top, finishing with a block (or with a stair once your blocks are used up). Nab: with secret targets, if someone walks through your square while you're hunting them, you grab them. In the Targets game everyone is dealt someone to take out, shown with a red ring, and you can only take out your own target; take them out and you inherit theirs. Take out half the table or be the last one standing to win. Taking someone out also gets you their unplayed blocks, and if you run out of blocks you simply stop building (a setting can make running out end the game, most takedowns winning). Once your pieces are used up (or none fit), you can climb one cube without a stair and each round your walk grows by one step.
          • Spider game. No targets. A spider that ignores gravity crawls over every face and the player on turn steers it. If it reaches you, you are out.
          • Undo. Until you end your turn you can undo your placements and your walk.
          • Looking around. Drag in any direction to tumble the world freely. With two fingers, pinch to zoom and twist to turn. Players hidden behind blocks show as a see-through silhouette. Scroll or pinch to zoom. The round arrow orbits on its own. The coloured buttons set whose down points down.
