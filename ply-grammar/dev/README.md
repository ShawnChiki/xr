# Ply Grammar: source parts and tests

`../index.html` is the built app. It is assembled from these parts:

- `head.html`: markup, styles, the settings diagrams (generated by `diag.py`)
- `pre.js`: default settings
- `geom.js`: geometry helpers (planes, polygons, Clipper)
- `ew_core.js`: the Edgewise XR core (`../../edgewise/core.js`, with one addition: `o.hold`, corners the solver must leave exactly where they are). `mkew.py` wraps it as `ew.js`, which `regen.py` puts into the page.
- `post.js`: everything else (panelizing, ribs, screws, nesting, build order, tests, headset)

## Build

    python3 mkew.py && python3 regen.py     # head.html + pre.js + geom.js + ew.js + post.js + net.js -> one page (edit the output path at the bottom of regen.py)
    python3 mkgh.py <built page> ../index.html   # wraps it as a full document with the password gate on

## Tests (Node, need `npm i three@0.160.0 clipper-lib@6.4.2` beside them)

    python3 mkh.py fz.js && node h.mjs 400     # torture test: 400 random obstacles through panelizing, overlap, build-order and fit checks
    FORCE=1 node h.mjs 150                     # same shapes with the two biggest parts of each split in two keyed pieces
    ROT=2 node h.mjs 200                       # everyday turned cut-outs: one cut-out, turned and sometimes tipped, on a simple box
    ROT=1 node h.mjs 400                       # wild: several turned and tipped cut-outs at once

The torture test also checks that every outside corner is filled (no socket without its finger) and that no plate is cut in two along a flat face.
It also walks a grid over every outside face and reports any see-through hole an inch square or more.
With those checks (v1.18): plain boxes 100% clean, boxes with cuts 99%, straight cut-outs 94%, everyday turned cut-outs 73% (the test got stricter in v1.15: a panel cut in two, or held by a neck under 5/8 in, now fails; under these checks v1.14 scored lower) (linings that cannot slide past ribs, and thin sharp triangle faces, are the open problems).
    python3 mkh.py test9.js && node h.mjs      # the original boxes
    python3 mkh.py cv.js && node h.mjs         # cut-outs: step, tunnel, sloped corner, 110" box
    python3 mkh.py dn.js && node h.mjs         # holes through the middle

v1.19: drawing the massing in the headset, the Edgewise way. `SK` is one Edgewise sketch (model inches) saved in the design as `sketch`; every closed solid in it is a block with `b.sk = {V: corners in the block's own space, F: faces as loops of corner ids}`. `skCells()` turns such a solid into convex pieces (its own face planes slice the space around it; cells inside the shape are kept and merged back where they stay convex), so `piecesOf()` / `brepOf()` and everything after them work unchanged. Solids that overlap or stand on each other are one block (`skTouch`). `skTidy()` squares a finished solid up (parallel, upright, level, square to its biggest wall, on the floor, whole inches with Snap on). `skPushFace()` is push / pull for a drawn face. In the headset: `skStroke*`, `skGrab*`, `skExtrude`, `skErase*`, and `xrJoint()` + the Panels-view rows of the wrist menu for joints and ribs.
Tests: `SK=1 node h.mjs 300` (after `python3 mkh.py fz.js`) runs drawn shapes (box, ramp, L, U, T, house, stairs, frustum, leaning box, a block standing on a block) through the torture test; `sk1.py`, `sk2.py`, `sk4.py`, `sk5.py` drive the pen, grab, eraser, undo, the joint menu and the desk tools with stand-in controllers (`drawn-shapes.json` is what `sk1.py` draws). `ldsk.js` loads a design with drawn blocks in node.
On 15 Nov 2026 (v1.19): plain boxes 100%, cuts 100%, straight cut-outs 95%; drawn shapes: box, ramp, leaning box, stairs 100%, L, T, house, frustum 97%, a block standing on a block 78%, U 52% (the misses are parts that cannot slide in: a wall that stands on a face with finger joints and is also held by a rib. `stepfix.js` shows which choice frees them; the Build tab's Find a fix now tries those: the inside corner simply meets, ribs respaced or turned, and it keeps going until nothing is stuck). Each block can now have its own rib direction (`ribAxis` on the block).

v1.20: inside (innie) corners get L-shaped corner blocks (`reflexPoly`): the two panels' backs face away from each other there, so the block wraps round behind the corner, one arm along each back, with no tabs (it goes in by hand once both panels are on) and screws from outside through each panel. `planAssembly` puts them in after both panels and never schedules a block before every part it holds on to. Headset: X undo, Y redo, B menu; Panels view trigger = quick tap picks a joint, a drag pushes faces and edges like massing (`xrFace` reads the massing face behind the part, `xrPreview` shows the box shape while dragging); grip there only moves the model; Build view on the wrist (`xrStepMsg`); cheaper Lambert shading for parts in the headset; "Working out the parts…" while it rebuilds. Pen: `skSplitStroke` turns a stroke with clear corners into joined lines, `skStraighten` merges a nearly straight continuation into one longer line. Figures (`fig.js`, pasted into post.js): standard body-segment proportions, joint limits, 13 poses, two-bone reach for hands and feet (`figReach` picks the reachable reading of each shoulder or hip turn), saved as `figures` in the design.
Tests: `sk8.py` (Panels-view push and tap), `sk9.py` (corner strokes, extensions), `sk10.py` (Build view menu), `fg1.py`-`fg4.py` (figure poses, desk drag, reach accuracy, headset add/pose/carry), `perf.py` (draw calls). Panelizing one of his boxes takes about 0.3 s on a desktop CPU and roughly a second on a Quest.

v1.21: a corner block is only kept if it is screwed from both of its panels. Three layers: a last-resort screw spot on each leg; a block that still cannot take a screw from both panels is taken back out and the next spot tried (joint blocks), or never placed (rib blocks pre-check a screw line through the face and through the rib); and `screwCheck(r)` runs on the finished parts after `deOverlap`, drops every screw that does not start in wood and end in another part's wood, then removes any block left one-sided and fills its tab slots back in. Legs are measured by how far they really bear on the plate (`lg-lo`), so a block clipped down to a point contact is rejected. `S.blocks` (default on) is the master switch for corner blocks (the old `S.cleats` is ignored, it was off in saved designs); `S.leanJoin` is `blocks` (default: rib blocks under leaning panels, and where none fits the rib tab runs right through and stands proud to be sanded) or `tabs`. Meet joints and off-square inside corners: the block grows a nub into the empty corner between the two panel ends (`x.nubs`; square inside corners keep the run-on instead). Blocks beside narrow faces can have one short leg (`asy`). A meet joint where no block fits is marked `bare` on the joint, and said so in the notes, the joint card and the joint menu. Desktop: a picked cut or cut-out shows move arrows in any tool, and a click picks the cut-out behind a cut-plane sheet; right-click near an edge (or click a joint in Panels) opens the joint menu (`jointNear`, `jointItems`, `jointMenu`). The explanations on every tab are hidden unless the switch on the (?) tab is on (`body.help`, `pgHelp`); the headset menu has its own "?". `fz.js` audits: block-screwed-one-side, block-no-screws, screw-into-air, meet-joint-no-block(explained); `SET='{"blocks":false}'` overrides settings for a run.

v1.22: corner blocks audited on the finished parts and reworked. `blkaudit.js` (spliced into `fz.js` by `mkfz.sh`; run with `BLK=1`) checks every block without trusting the code that planned it: what it bears on, each screw's bite, wood either side, head distance from panel edges, crossing screws, driver access at its build step, shape (slivers, necks), sticking out of the shape, and how many of a joint's planned blocks it got. `SECT=dir` with a verbose seed (or `DESIGN=file.json`) writes a cross-section picture of every block; `sheet.py dir out.png` makes the contact sheet. `WHY=1` tallies why block-less joints have none. Rules now shared by both panelizers: `screwRunOK` (all of the screw past its panel is in the block, with wood either side and past the tip), `legScrewSpots` (one each side of the tab; a leg with no room for a tab and a screw keeps the screw), `mkSolid`/`solidsClash`/`headUnder` (a block keeps out of every block already placed, rib blocks included, and off their screw heads), `blockStout` (no slivers or necks). Blocks may have one short leg beside a rib or panel; tabs narrower than the ply are dropped; a third panel only counts as a side if it stands square to the block. Rib clearance is direction-aware (`trav`): a tabbed block needs back-off room only toward a rib; where there is none a plain screwed block (`nt`) is tried. A joint is filled to its planned count with stand-in spots (`fbSort`: kind by kind, furthest-from-neighbours first); each joint records why spots failed (`b.blkWhy`, `j.why`) and block-less joints say so in the notes. On shapes with inside corners a far face's plane no longer cuts a leg off (only faces standing over the block clip it), which is what left drawn T and step shapes with no inside-corner blocks. `reflexPoly` is a clean mitred L. The meet-joint nub is gone (the two panels touch along their inside corners, so it could never attach); the inside-corner nub stays. Ribs: `ribRoom` nudges a rib clear of walls and corners it runs beside, and one jammed within 1.6 ply of a wall is left out. Rib blocks plan tabs and screws before cutting anything, keep to their side of every other rib, and always go in on their rib (bench). `planAssembly`: a block comes out only while one of its panels is off (so tabless blocks are not scheduled after the shape is closed); three passes. `screwCheck` also drops block screws that bite under half an inch or sit within 0.22 in of a panel edge. Hand edits: `cleatFreeze` pins a joint's blocks the first time one is touched, so stand-in blocks can be slid and deleted. Build text knows tabless, L-shaped and symmetric blocks; rib-side screws show in their step.

v1.23: the build planner (`planAssembly`) no longer gives up on a panel that cannot leave in one straight line. For each part it now tries, in order: one straight move (as before); a plain slide along the panel's own plane (`asmAlong`: the exact directions of its two longest outline edges; a 5° rounding here used to drift a long tunnel wall into its ribs); then two moves (`pick.seat = {d, s}`): eased off its tabs square to itself by 1.45 or 2.6 ply, then drawn out another way (`asmSweepFrom`). Put together that reads "lower it in an inch to one side of its place, then push it flat onto its tabs". The Build view plays both legs (`AS.seat`, 1.3 s in + 0.7 s push), draws a short pink arrow for the push (`AS.arrow2`) and says it in the step text. In the shell panelizer a panel's inside-corner run-on now stops a ply short at an outline corner where the next inside joint belongs to the other panel (`backS`/`backE`), which is what locked the channel walls of U shapes together. Planner speed: what blocked a move is remembered while that part is still in (`memo`), and each part's test points are sorted along its longest side so `asmHit` only walks the stretch beside the neighbour (`sk`, `asmLow`); a 61-part shape plans in 1.8 s instead of 4.4 s, with identical plans. Drill room: `AS.tight[k]` counts the screws of step k with less than `DRILL_ROOM` (7 in) of clear air in front of the head, given what is built by then; those are drawn orange, the step says to use a stubby or right-angle driver, and the Build summary lists the steps.
Tools: `two.sh design.json PART "OTHERS"` tries the two-move exit of one part by hand, `coll.sh design.json "A7:AR2:n" ...` prints where a part first collides, `SLICEP="o;u;v;name|..."` / `SLICEJ="7>10,.5"` on a verbose seed write section pictures through any plane or joint (`slice2png.py name...` turns them into PNGs), `ORDER=1` prints the build order, `bld.py design.json [steps]` prints every Build step (`SCRUB=250` screenshots a step part-way through).
On 24 Nov 2026 (v1.23): plain boxes 100%, cuts 100%, straight cut-outs 97% (1 stuck in 154); drawn shapes: U 100% (was 52%), step 94% (was 81%), L and T 97%, the rest 100%; everyday turned cut-outs 82% clean, 6 stuck in 120 (was 73%, 19 stuck). What is still stuck is real: two ribs that cross inside a closed tunnel hole (a half-lap cannot be assembled there), and a turned wall that stands in finger slots in the base while also passing under a rib.

v1.24: phone and headset together, the Quadrille XR way (`net.js`, appended after post.js). Devices meet through the artifact's `room` in the Claude copy, or by a 4-digit room number over PeerJS on GitHub Pages (`netPeerRoom`: one device claims `ply-grammar-<room>` and relays; `turn-config.js` in the head adds the relay; joining waits for the password). One design for all: every saved change is stamped (`NET.st` = time + device, kept in `pgSync`) and the newest wins; designs travel as `d` messages in 1200-character pieces, a device that sees a newer stamp in someone's presence asks with `need`. A design that arrives goes through `restoreModel(d,{remote,lite,live})` and onto the undo list; it waits while something is being dragged here (`netBusy`). While dragging, the design also goes out about 4 times a second marked live (others show it as massing until the final one). The headset's presence carries its head, both controllers (grip and pointer poses, buttons, pointer length, input profiles), the wrist menu (message, tool, page, hover), the live pen stroke, its view and build step; flat screens send their camera in model space. A flat screen that sees a headset draws it in `NET.so.hs`, a group whose matrix is the inverse of the headset's model transform, so head, controllers (the real Quest models from the same profiles, with the button labels, or the simple ones in the artifact) and the wrist menu sit where they are relative to the obstacle at life size or tabletop; it follows the headset's view and build step when they change; Around it / Their eyes / Both (`netRender`). The headset draws each flat camera as an outline (`netFrusta`) and shows what the phone sees on a small screen beside the wrist menu (`netPreview`, every third frame). Wrist menu: a Together row (room, who is here; on GitHub it opens a number pad, `XR.step='room'`) and Phone view. Together tab on the rail; a pill on the stage switches how you watch.
Tests: `tg1.py` (two browser pages bridged as one artifact room: join, edits both ways, a live drag, undo, a stand-in headset to watch, the headset side; `PV=1` takes the preview shots), `tg2.py` (the GitHub copy on two devices through a local PeerJS server from the `peer` package).
