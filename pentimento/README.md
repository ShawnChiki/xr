# Pentimento XR

Sketch skate terrain in VR or MR with as many loose, overlapping lines as you like. Mark the edges and the lines that matter, and one smooth quad surface is found through all of them.

Open in the Quest browser: https://shawnchiki.github.io/xr/pentimento/

## Versions

- **v2.9** — Guide lines (the blue topology lines) no longer leave holes in the cage, and folded or overlapping faces are mostly gone: the flattening step keeps shapes truer, guides that run right alongside a crease are skipped, and guides that stop just short of the border are carried out to it. The Surface tab shows the current quad count ("now 1,240") next to Target quads. The test bench has hand-drawn guide cases that check for holes.
- **v2.8** — The flowing quad layout from 2.7 left small glitches (slivers and folded faces) on real sketches, so the steady layout is back as the default; the flowing one is an opt-in (Surface → More settings → Quads: Flowing (beta)). The test bench now counts glitches too. Size lock: two hands move and turn the model without scaling it (Sketch tab next to Model size, or the B dial). While both hands hold the model, a ruler between them shows the real distance and the model size; when scaling, the model rests on the standard sizes with a click.
- **v2.7** — A sturdier builder. Creases and edges now form one network: ends that nearly meet become one shared corner, a line that stops on another splits it there, and averaged crease passes at a corner no longer merge into one line (an L-shaped ledge drawn as averaged creases went from 8 cm off at the corner to under 1 cm). After the creases are cut in, each patch between them is fitted from its own lines. Quads are now laid out by a field-aligned remesher (direction and grid fields that run along creases, the border and your guides): inner vertices that don't have four edges fell from about 15–20% to 3–11% on the test shapes, so the mesh is cleaner to edit; if it can't cover the surface cleanly the builder falls back to the old layout. A Trust look (View tab) colours the surface by how well it agrees with your lines. A yellow ring marks a strict line end that stops just short of another line, and ends near another line's body now snap onto it. Small changes rebuild from the last fit (the rough fit is skipped). Builder: Classic (2.6) in Surface → More settings builds exactly as 2.6 did. A test bench (tests/bench.js) scores 23 hard cases on every change; problem reports dropped in tests/reports become tests. Save OBJ moved to the Files tab, Make strict to the Layers tab.
- **v2.6** — Inside corners: where an edge between two lines blocked the next crease (the inside corner of an L-shaped ledge, where two outside top edges meet an inside vertical edge), the crease used to stop short and the corner filled in with a rounded web; it now runs right into the corner (on a test ledge, 2–3 cm off at the corner became 0.2–0.3 cm). Lines next to the end of a crease, where three or more sheets meet, are no longer forced onto one of two sides. When a build fails, it finds the line that breaks it (adding your newest lines back in halves), builds everything without it and flashes that line pink. Each end of a strict crease can be set Sharp, Soft or Very soft: hold the end point and press B (alt-click on a computer); light-blue and white end points are soft and very soft. A quick tap of A goes back to Draw.
- **v2.5** — Sharp shapes hold up. Lines on the two sides of a sharp crease are kept apart while fitting (each loose line near a crease is sorted onto one side, a whole stroke voting), so a spine's two walls are never averaged into one hump; on test spines the error near the top fell from 9–15 cm to about 3 cm. A strict crease the first fit didn't reach (a thin spine top) now pulls the sheet up to it before it is cut in, and a crease that can't be cut in at one spot keeps going past the gap instead of being dropped whole (this was a common silent failure). Fold repair no longer smooths the faces right at a crease. If a build breaks, it retries more simply (without the newer extras, then with strict lines as averaged ones, then without creases) and says what it skipped. After a failed build, Last good (in place of Redo, or Y) puts the sketch back to the last version that built in one press.
- **v2.4** — Menu styles (View tab): Full, Tidy (tools on the A dial in the headset, main settings first, the rest under More settings) and Compact (a small card that opens one tab at a time; the B dial picks line types). The menu is now only as tall as what it shows. Dials have icons. Bend moves every line inside its sphere, loose and strict, fading toward the edge. Flatten is crease-aware: where a crease runs through the sphere, each side settles onto its own surface through the crease, so the fold stays sharp (in a test, 1.3 cm off the true shape instead of 3.1 cm). Crease ends (Surface tab): a sharp crease that stops in the open fades out over its last stretch (Sharp, Soft, Very soft). Problem report (Files tab): one file with the sketch, all settings, recent messages and a picture of your view, to attach in a chat with Claude.
- **v2.3** — Quads from your guides: set Target quads to Guides ×1, ×4 or ×16 and draw guides on the surface where the quad edges should run. The guides, creases and the border cut the surface into patches; each patch becomes one face (or 4, or 16), creases stay sharp, and guides that stop just short of a line or the border are stretched to meet it. This is the second step of a two-step workflow: rough loose lines, Flatten and Smooth, and strict creases get the shape; guides then give a very light, editable SubD layout. "On surface" (Sketch tab) makes guides, or every line, land on the built surface as you draw. One kind of guide (Strict guides become Guides). Hiding a layer now hides its laid guides and averaged lines too. Strict-line points click together when dragged near each other (with a haptic knock), points that sit together move together, and a new strict line's ends click onto points already there. Clear all is on the Sketch tab. New Files tab: save sketches to a list in the browser, load or delete them, download a sketch as a file and open it again. Builds that fail now say so on the Build button and keep the last surface; a build that runs over a minute stops with a hint. OBJ export handles faces with any number of sides.
- **v2.2** — The page checks for a newer version every minute and a half (and when it opens) and offers a one-tap update, in the menu's title bar and on the start card, so a stale copy in the browser never hides a new version. The surface builder file always matches the page's version.
- **v2.1** — Sharp corners on strict lines: sharp turns you draw become corner points automatically (yellow), and any point can be switched between smooth and sharp; corners stay sharp all the way through the surface. Control points move with the grip (middle finger) instead of the trigger; gripping a strict line between points adds one; while holding a point, A/X deletes it and B/Y makes it a sharp corner. Green Edge and Strict edge lines say where the surface ends (a closed loop keeps its inside). Surface tab reorganised like a quad remesher: Target quads (Auto, 50–3200) rebuilds the result as a simpler quad layout on the fitted surface, and Smart creases turns folds sharper than 30°/45°/60° into creases. The smooth result now hugs the fitted surface.
- **v2.0** — Blue guides now steer instead of shape: the surface comes from loose lines and creases only; guides are laid onto it, smoothed, and set the direction the quads run (in a test, edges lined up with the guides went from 45% to 68%) and the direction Relax smooths. Quads are squared up toward that flow. Smooth is now a line tool like Flatten: it irons the wobble out of loose lines and guides inside the sphere. Flatten keeps curves: it fits the full curvature and no longer compounds flattening (on a tight transition with a big sphere it used to pull lines 14 cm toward flat; now about 0). The sensed patch shows as a curved yellow mesh. Changing anything while the surface is building stops that build and starts over with the new lines.
- **v1.9** — Dial menus on the right hand. Hold A for the tool dial (Draw, Bend, Flatten, Smooth, Mark, Erase bits, Erase lines); hold B for the panel dial (Sketch, Layers, Surface and View panels, plus show/hide tips, surface, lines and see room / full VR). Push the stick toward a slice, or move your hand that way, and let go of the button to pick. Undo and redo moved to X and Y on the left hand.
- **v1.8** — Creases take priority over smoothing: Smoothness, smooth paint (built and live preview) and Relax no longer round a crease off (at Smoothness 10 a test ridge kept 81–85° of its 84° fold, where before it flattened to 5°). Smoothness at its highest still listens to your lines. New Relax slider (Surface tab, 0–10, default 2) settles the finished mesh while every guide and crease stays put.
- **v1.7** — Mesh edges view (View tab, Surface tab or Q key): Off, Quads (the new quad layout that Save OBJ exports, with edges running along the creases) or Fine (the subdivided mesh you see). Works over Shaded and Zebra.
- **v1.6** — Switch between MR and VR any time from the Room button at the top of the menu (or View → Background → Your room); both Enter buttons open the same kind of session, so there's no leaving and re-entering. Surface on/off button in the menu's top row (S key): while it's off nothing rebuilds, so sketching and flattening run at full speed, and turning it on builds once. Auto/Manual build moved to the Surface tab. Faster smooth-paint tinting.
- **v1.5** — Strict lines are splines: control points appear as soon as you draw one (or press Make strict); grab a point with Draw or Bend and drag, and the line stays smooth through the points. Bend on a spline moves nearby points together. View tab: lighting presets (Studio, Low sun with shadows, Soft, Headlamp), Zebra stripes for checking smoothness, and Dark / Light / Sky backgrounds. Fixed: hovering with Bend no longer hides lines.
- **v1.4** — Loops: a line that goes round more than once (or scrubs back and forth) is cut into one piece per lap, when you draw it and again when the surface builds. Smooth paint: paint over lumpy surface to smooth it, live; the paint is kept with the sketch as its own layer so that area keeps building smoother (Erase bits removes it, Layers has Clear). Lines button and A button hide every drawn line (A cycles surface + lines, surface only, lines only). Tips in their own panel.
- **v1.3** — Flatten tool: sweep a sphere through your lines; it senses their average surface (a gentle curved patch, so bowls stay bowls) and smoothly eases the loose lines onto it. Strict and locked lines stay put and steer it. A disc shows the sensed surface; the buzz fades as lines settle. Surface building now trusts the bulk of your lines over ones that floated off or overshot. The sample sketch now looks like real VR sketching: lines float, drift in depth, cut corners and overshoot.
- **v1.2** — Erasing shows up instantly: rubbed-out bits and erased lines vanish the same frame, and the controller buzzes in proportion to how much you're erasing. Bend shows the line moving live. Version number on the splash card and the hand menu.
- **v1.1** — Five line types (Loose, Guide, Strict guide, Crease, Strict crease). Strict lines are used exactly as drawn and stay exact through subdivision. Layers tab with show and lock per type. Bend tool. Make strict turns averaged guides and creases into strict lines. Fewer folds on messy sketches.
- **v1.0** — First version: draw, mark and erase tools; averaged guides and creases; crease-aware quad retopology with Catmull-Clark subdivision; OBJ export.
