# Pentimento Park

The all-in-one skatepark builder for Quest: record fingerboard runs, sketch terrain around them, surface it,
and watch the runs ride the surface. Built from Pentimento XR (v2.20) with Party Lines recording brought inside.

Planned phases: record + sketch + surface (now) → SubD editing (smoothing, moving points, manual patching) →
panelization for fabrication (concrete and wood approaches) → export to Rhino and Blender.

## Changelog

- **v4.1** — Fab → 3D print: the park cut into solid chunks that fit your print bed (bed size and height in mm; 256 × 256 × 256 to start). The seams run along the SubD's own quad lines (with and across the flow, so they curve like the surface does), the coping and other creases are joints from the start, and a region too big for the bed is split by the run of a quad line across its middle, short and square to its length, finishing on a seam that's already there, so pieces stagger instead of making a grid. Crumbs along the edge join a neighbour. Pieces too tall for the bed are cut across where the cut is shortest (usually through a wall). Each chunk is a closed solid: its piece of the surface, straight walls down its seams, a flat bottom at the base. Coloured and numbered, together or pulled apart; Save STLs gives a zip with one STL per chunk in mm, each turned to lie square on the bed. Chunks also go to Rhino on a Print chunks layer.
- **v4.0** — Layers. Sketch → Layers: every line belongs to a layer, and each layer builds its own surface. You draw, build, shape (SubD) and fab on the layer marked Drawing; the others keep the surface they last built (or as you last shaped it) and only rebuild, in the background, when their own lines or the build settings change. Layers overlap wherever you put them (no blending yet), each has a slight tint of its colour, can be shown or hidden, and in Play you ride them all (with coping and the apron round the lot). + New layer, Delete layer (its lines go too; Undo brings them back). Layers are saved with the sketch, in the library and files, travel to friends in a room, and go to Rhino as separate surface layers. What used to be called layers (Loose, Guide, Crease… shown / locked / hidden) is now Show or lock line types.
- **v3.20** — Radius check only marks inside curves too tight to ride: transitions and bowl corners where a fingerboard's nose or tail would hit the surface before its wheels sit down. The limit defaults to what a 96 mm fingerboard can ride (about 2⅜″ in the piece, worked out from its wheelbase, length and kicks), and you can still set your own. Outside curves (hips, rounded edges) never count.
- **v3.19** — Fab in four tabs: The piece (units, scale, sheets, piece limit, thickness fit, base), Panels, Waffle and Export (Rhino, OBJ), one at a time; the finer panel settings (cell sizes, panels per strip, fold turn, seams, joining) sit behind More settings. The waffle doesn't need panels, and the Waffle tab can show it alone, with the surface or with the panels. Radius check in Sketch and Surface (on by default): the surface turns orange to red where it bends tighter than a radius you set (5″ by default, in the piece at Fab's build scale), with a callout giving the radius at the tightest spot of each patch; creases don't count.
- **v3.18** — Play: Land assist (More settings, on by default, Subtle or Strong). In the air it looks ahead along your fall; if you'd come down flat into a slope or into a wall, it tries small sideways nudges and leans your flight toward the one that lands you rolling along a surface going your way (a transition, a bank), re-planning every tenth of a second so it feels like you aimed it. In a test of 30 drops into the sample park, Strong made landings about a quarter softer.
- **v3.17** — Play: the right stick turns you smoothly, the way you push it (it was a 30° snap, and backwards). Coping is chunkier and louder when you grind it, and easier to catch: falling toward a seam from an ollie you drift onto it, and only a properly upside-down landing bails. A bail puts you straight back on the board where you fell, upright and a bit slower, instead of back at the start. Sketch: new lines get control points 1.5× further apart (fewer points to manage); lines you already drew keep theirs.
- **v3.16** — Play scale flipped to how it reads: 1:10 is a real board in a real park (a fingerboard on the 1:10 model), and a bigger number is a smaller you, so 1:20 makes the park feel twice as big, up to 1:40. New On the surface choice while you skate: Plain, SubD quads (the quad layout drawn on the surface) or Panels (your Fab panels in place of the surface).
- **v3.15** — Play: nothing turns the board on its own. Tilt carving is off for everyone (a fresh setting, so an old saved choice can't switch it back on), and the left stick has a bigger dead zone so stick drift can't steer you.
- **v3.14** — Play fixes. Bailing no longer stops the game (the respawn lost track of your feet for a frame). Steering is back to pointing your hands (your feet) the way to go, plus the left stick: forward carves toward your toes, back toward your heels; tilting the controllers no longer carves (it read the way you hold them as a lean and sent you in circles; it's an option under More settings). Right stick up / down changes the board size while you skate. Grinding is easier: more seams count (folds from 25°), the tops of walls where your surface ends are coping too, and you catch a seam from further away and less lined up. If skating ever hits an error it says so and B starts you again, instead of freezing. The controller's undo / redo arrows keep trying until the controller's buttons have loaded. Fab: Flat to within is now Gap to the surface, in real fractions (1/32″ to 1/2″, or mm), with a note on what it and Bend each do.
- **v3.13** — Play together. Files → Play together: Host a room gives a 4-digit code; friends press Join a room and type it on the keypad (PeerJS, like Negative Space and ChikiBlox, up to 8 people). Lines, smooth paint, runs and the build settings are shared, and each headset builds the surface itself from the same lines. Everyone keeps their own step, model size and view, so one person can skate in Play while another sketches. Others show as a coloured head (eyes, and a mouth that opens when they talk), two hands and a tag saying which step they're on, at their own size: a skater is a little person on your 1:10 model, a sketcher a giant head over a skater's park. When they skate you see their board. Voice chat: everyone hears everyone, each voice coming from where that person is (never more than a few steps away, so tiny and giant friends are easy to hear); Mic on / off. Your undo only takes back your own changes: other people's edits are folded into your undo history as they arrive. Only the host can open, clear or load a sketch, and when they do everyone gets it. If the host leaves, everyone keeps their copy. SubD shaping and Fab aren't shared yet.
- **v3.12** — New step 7, Play: skate your park with physics. Press SKATE and your hands are your feet on a real-size board (the park grows round you), or pick 1:10, 1:15, 1:20 or 1:25 to ride a fingerboard on the model you'll build at that scale. The ride is Subdeck XR's (trigger booster toward where you look, ollie with A or by snapping the tail down, bails, live sound), with better trucks: lean both hands toward your toes or heels to carve (never tighter than the wheels can grip at that speed), the board sits on both trucks so it bridges transitions, and the deck leans and the trucks turn as you carve. Sharp convex seams are coping you can grind (drop on from an ollie, or roll onto one lined up; auto grind pops you up onto one beside you). Past your surface's open edge an apron carries on at the edge's own slope, levels out into a deck, then ramps down to a big plain, all in its own blue-grey, so you roll off and back on without a lip to catch. Starts: the edge nearest you, then where each run starts (X for the next, B to go back). Left stick press pauses and shows the menu, Y stops. On a computer: W, A / D, S, Space, R, N, P, Esc. More settings has Booster, Top speed, Grip, Ollie pop, Rolling drag, and switches for lean, auto grind and sound. Also: Undo and Redo are now blue arrow buttons, apart from the Lines / Surface / Runs switches, and the controller's undo / redo buttons wear bold curved arrows; the Layers card is back in plain view in Sketch.
- **v3.11** — Out to Rhino. Save Rhino file (.3dm) puts everything in one file on named layers, at the size you'll build it (mm, Z up, base at 0): your lines by type, the surface, its SubD control net (ToSubD in Rhino) and its crease edges, the quad surface, the creases, the runs, the panels in place (named, coloured and grouped by strip, with name dots), the waffle as solid closed pieces (named), and every cut sheet laid flat beside the model on CUT / SCORE_FRONT / SCORE_BACK / LABELS / SHEETS layers. Cut sheets and ribs also save as DXF (mm, same layers) next to the SVGs. Rhino's own library (rhino3dm) is fetched the first time.
- **v3.10** — Waffle: Follow the quads. Ribs run under every control edge of the surface, dropped straight down to the base, so they follow the flow (across transitions, radial round a bowl corner, along the coping) and panels and coping land on a rib. Each rib is an edge loop of the control quads (straight on through every point where four quads meet), a vertical wall over its line in plan, so it unrolls flat exactly; it breaks where the line turns sharply in plan or climbs a near-vertical wall. Loops crossing at a point slot together (the longer one from below). Same stock limits, staggered joints and stacked layers as the grid. Rib tops follow the surface more smoothly in both modes.
- **v3.9** — The waffle shows as solid parts, the thickness of the rib sheet, each piece its own shade of wood. Every piece fits the stock (a 4′ × 8′ sheet at the build scale, or the cut sheet): along a rib, joints go halfway between crossing ribs so each piece is held by its slots, staggered like brickwork from one rib to the next and from one layer to the next. A waffle taller than the stock is built in stacked layers, each its own egg-crate with slots half that layer's height, so no piece gets cut apart by its own slots. Rib pieces are named rib then letter (X3A, X3B …), and the SVG cuts each piece's outline, slots included, in one line.
- **v3.8** — Fit for thickness (on by default): the riding face sits on the drawn surface and the sheet hangs under it, so where two pieces meet over a convex bend each edge is set back by t·tan(θ/2) and the undersides don't collide; every fold gets its kerf, 2t·tan(θ/2) wide (two score lines where that's worth cutting), green to score on the back (convex folds), blue on the front (concave). Names read strip number then panel letter (12C: strip 12, third panel), in both Strips and Voronoi. Pieces are capped at a full 4′ × 8′ sheet at the build scale (9.6″ × 4.8″ at 1:10), or optionally at the cut sheet.
- **v3.7** — New panel type for laser-cut ply: Strips. Ribbons run along rows of the surface's own quads, the rows that bend most taking their quads first (up a transition, down a bowl's wall), so each strip bends along its length. It folds straight across itself on every crease it crosses and wherever it would bend more than the sheet allows (Bend) or past flat; folds are scored, the outline is cut. Narrow strips are a row of the surface's quads, Wide strips a row of its control quads (twice as wide); the quad size on step 3 sets the width, and guides steer the rows. Run with the bend, or across it. Each strip is unrolled flat (lengths kept), split to fit the sheet, named per panel, with its own SVG and OBJ.
- **v3.6** — Voronoi cells end cleanly on creases: the surface is now cut open along the build's own crease edges (strict and averaged), one copy of each crease point per side, instead of guessing seams from how much the normals differ (which left gaps and spiky cells where a crease softens toward its tip). Strip colours at full saturation.
- **v3.5** — Fab in inches or millimetres: a Units switch at the top of The piece, and every size there reads in it (tape-measure fractions to 1/32″ in inches). Panels and ribs each have their own sheet thickness, from 1/32″ (or 0.5 mm) up to 3/4″. New Bend setting for Voronoi panels, 0 to 1: at 0 every panel stays flat; above 0 a panel may bend with the sheet, down to a radius of about 150 × its thickness at 1, so cells can be bigger where the surface only curves one way. Strips: set Panels per strip (default 12) and Strip folds turn at most (default 10°) before making them; a strip keeps one bend grain, every fold within that angle of the fold before it, and ends where the grain turns. Panels are coloured by strip (By cell and How flat are still there).
- **v3.4** — Irregular points go to flat ground. After the seams are cleaned up, every triangle left where the surface bends is tried moving off each of its three sides, and goes wherever it lands flattest (or meets another triangle and makes a quad of both). On the test bench, faces that aren't quads went from 677 to 501, and the share of the leftovers sitting on flat ground (under 8° of bend) from 47% to 70%; on the half pipe none are left on the transition. In SubD, points you carry with an edge or face (moved or pulled out) click onto any point close by, including a neighbour, and merge when you let go; only a neighbour you started on top of (an extruded copy's own edge) is skipped.
- **v3.3** — Fewer triangles on the seams again, in both quad layouts. A triangle on a crease whose far corner isn't on any line (or sits where a line ends) is now pushed off the crease too, with its two new corners spread so the new quad comes out square. Flowing quads (beta, Surface → More settings → Fine tuning) now get the same crease-band rebuild and triangle pushing as Even. A crease band's end rung with fewer edges than the band has rows is split, and the extra corner passed out past the band's end. On the test bench, triangles on seams went from 169 to 147 (Even) and 171 to 142 (Flowing), with no more folds. Even stays the default: Flowing still makes far more triangles overall and folds more on the sample sketches.
- **v3.2** — Two strict creases running close together get a clean band between them: straight rungs from one crease to the other, one to three rows of quads deep, instead of loose points and folded or skewed faces. Where one crease has more points along the stretch, the extra corners are passed out away from the creases. Triangles sitting on a crease are pushed off it and paired up where there's room, so there are far fewer triangles on the seams (on the test bench, triangles touching a crease went from 389 to 169, all faces that aren't quads from 831 to 695, with the shape unchanged). In SubD, an edge you pull out to fill a gap clicks onto points close by while you drag it, and merges when you let go. Pressing Ride scales the park to 1:10 from its edge nearest you, so it no longer swings through you after opening a file. Looking at your empty menu hand shows a tip: tap the trigger for the menu.
- **v3.1** — Fab is ready for laser-cut ply. Hexes are now Voronoi panels: cells come out small where the surface curves tightly and big where it's flat, between a Smallest and Largest cell you set, and "Flat to within" decides where between. Creases split the surface first, so each seam is one clean edge. Every panel gets a name (strip letter + place, like BM4; turn on Names to see them on the surface), and neighbouring panels stay joined in unrolled strips (Panels per strip) that fit your sheet: Save cut sheets SVG lays them out on sheets with red cut lines, blue dashed score lines where a strip folds, and the names. A new "The piece" card at the top of Fab sets the build scale (1:10 by default), sheet thickness (1/8" default), sheet size and base, and shows the overall size. Waffle underneath: ribs both ways at a spacing, from the base up to just under the panels, with half-depth slots where they cross; ribs longer than the sheet are split between crossings. Save ribs SVG.
- **v3.0** — Averaged (orange) creases and edges are drawn with control points now, like strict ones, and their ends snap onto other lines' points. Surface has a Creases card: Crease (averaged) strength from Gentle to Sharp (how sharp the orange creases come out), Crease tips (how a crease that stops in the open fades), Shortest crease edge and Crease rows. For one tip only, grip a crease's end point and press B.
- **v2.9** — SubD Grab now follows Gravity Sketch's controls: grip (middle finger) grabs what's lit (a point, edge or face), or with nothing lit, every point inside the grab sphere (stick up / down sizes it). While holding, the trigger extrudes: a border edge pulls out into a new face, a face pulls out with sides, and each pull adds another row; A deletes what you hold. Not holding: trigger on an edge loop-cuts, trigger on points or in mid-air places corners of a new face (four make one); B creases.
- **v2.8** — Fab panels, redone. Quads: flat panels along the surface's own quad lines; each quad stays one panel if it's within Flat to within (3 mm to 8 cm), otherwise it splits in four until every piece is, and four flat quads round a point join into one big panel. Edges follow the quad lines, so they're clean, and creases are always panel edges. Hexes: one size (15 cm up), straight edges between the points where three panels meet, each on its own plane. Save flat panels OBJ gives each panel as one flat polygon, named with how much it bows.
- **v2.6** — Edit your runs' paths in the Ride step: Points shows control points along each path (drag one and the path bends smoothly between its neighbours), Smooth sweeps a sphere along a path to iron out wobbles (the board's tilt evens out too, flips left alone), and each run row has a Smooth button for the whole run. Every edit undoes.
- **v2.5** — In Sketch, tapping B goes back to Loose (as tapping A goes back to Draw). Surface → More → Side-by-side creases: even rows (beta) puts evenly spaced lines between two creases that run side by side a few quads apart; it helps some sketches and can leave a glitch in others, so it's off unless you turn it on.
- **v2.4** — Fewer triangles: after the quads are laid out, pairs of triangles joined by a straight strip of quads are turned into all quads (the strip's edges shift over by one); on the sample sketches about half the triangles go. Guides now do something you can see: they're drawn with points you can drag, like strict lines, and the quads' edges run along them. An averaged crease that ends near a strict one sticks to it (onto its point if one is close, otherwise onto the line), easing in over its last stretch. Ride lists each run with its own Delete. Surface → Shortest crease edge sets how short edges may get where a strict crease bends (Even keeps one size everywhere). When the Quest switches to hand tracking, the app ignores the hands and the menu stays put where it was until a controller comes back.
- **v2.3** — Fab step: Hex panels. Pick a panel size and press Make panels: the surface is covered in panels of that size whatever its mesh density, relaxed until they settle into hexagons, with creases as seams (a toggle lets panels cross them). Colour by panel, or by how flat each one is (green: cut from flat sheet; red: needs bending). Save panels OBJ gives one object per panel for Rhino (Smash / UnrollSrf) or Blender.
- **v2.2** — Redraw edges is two steps now: sweep along the edges you want to move (they turn blue; B starts the pick over), then draw where they should go. The old one-step version could hang the headset on long curves; it's gone, and a SubD tool that hits a problem now says so instead of freezing the app.
- **v2.1** — SubD works like Gravity Sketch: the new Grab tool (the default) lights up whatever you point at, a point, an edge or a face. Trigger drags it (a point dropped on another merges), A deletes it (an edge between two faces joins them), B creases it, a stick press loop-cuts through an edge; on a computer, hover and drag, Delete, C and L. Zebra, Trust and the mesh edge views moved to Settings → Surface look. The Build button says how long the last build took. Hiding the lines no longer hides the runs.
- **v2.0** — Strict creases no longer crowd the quads: along a straightish strict line the edges are as long as anywhere else, and they only get shorter where it bends (so the density is even, crease or no crease). - **v1.9** — Redraw edges (SubD step): draw a curve along a row of edges, or where you want the row to go. - **v1.8** — Step 4 is now called SubD. - **v1.7** — Shape gets Cut: tap an edge for a new point on it, tap points or edges one after another to cut new edges across the faces (optionally as creases, for sharp seams); tap the last point again to stop.
